## Which one do you need?

You can use py61850 under the **AGPL, for free**, if:

- you use it **inside your own organisation** and publish nothing. Internal
  commissioning tools, engineering scripts, a substation HMI your own staff use
  — the AGPL's obligations are triggered by conveying software to others, not
  by using it. A utility running py61850 on its own network owes nothing.
- you **distribute** software built on it and are willing to release that whole
  work under the AGPL, source included, to the people you distribute it to.
- you **host** a service built on it and are willing to offer the complete
  source of that service to its users (AGPL section 13).

You need a **commercial licence** if:

- you want to **ship a closed-source product** that embeds or imports py61850 —
  an HMI, a test set, a gateway, a configuration tool, a vendor SCADA package;
- you want to **run a hosted or SaaS service** built on py61850 without
  publishing its source to your users;
- you need it under terms the AGPL does not offer — a warranty, an indemnity,
  liability limits your own contracts require, or the ability to sublicense.

Selling is allowed under the AGPL. What the AGPL does not allow is keeping the
source closed. That is the line the commercial licence crosses.

## What a commercial licence covers

Terms are negotiated per case; they typically settle:

- **Scope** — which products, which sites, how many installations or devices.
- **Fee** — a one-off, an annual subscription, per-installation, or a share of
  the revenue of the product py61850 goes into.
- **Duration and updates** — which versions the licence covers, and for how long.
- **Support** — whether any is included, and at what response time.
- **Warranty and liability** — the AGPL disclaims both; a commercial agreement
  can allocate them.

Open an issue at
<https://github.com/GuilhermeMarini/py61850/issues> saying what you want to
build, or write to the copyright holder directly:

Please describe the product, the deployment shape (shipped software, hosted
service, embedded device) and the rough scale. That is enough for a first
answer on terms.
